# Appendix figures

Captured offscreen with headless Chrome from the checksummed `explorer.html`, at 1440px
with the persistent header, footer and chapter navigation suppressed. Reproduce with
`make_figures.sh`. The figures document interface mechanics; none of them is evidence
about the incident.

## Figure B.1 — The evidence boundary
`B1_evidence_boundary.png` · route `#/evidence`

> **Figure B.1.** The evidence chapter. The dashed rule separates what the reviewed sources
> establish from where they stop; the two unresolved questions on the right are the ones the
> conditional layer depends on. The callout states the encoding rule directly: absence of
> evidence about a control is recorded as *unknown*, not as absence of the control.
> The figure shows the interface's treatment of the boundary, not evidence that readers
> perceive it — one of two participants reported initial difficulty with this distinction.

## Figure B.2 — Propagation of an unresolved input
`B2a_unknown_baseline.png` (baseline) · `B2b_unknown_after_change.png` (one setting changed)
· route `#/pathway`

> **Figure B.2.** Three-valued propagation. *Left:* the evidence-limited baseline. Every
> conditional step reads *cannot be determined*, and the model names the first step it cannot
> settle rather than returning a verdict. *Right:* one setting — independent publication
> authorisation — moved from *Unknown* to *No verification*. That step becomes *occurs*
> (solid), the change is recorded in the strip at the foot of the page, and the downstream
> steps remain *cannot be determined*: resolving one input does not resolve the pathway.
> The right-hand panel is a selected assumption, not a finding.

## Figure B.3 — The matched governance comparison
`B3_governance_comparison.png` · route `#/safeguards/governance`

> **Figure B.3.** The governance chapter. Both columns hold the same consequence and the same
> core scenario. As the interface states beneath them, they differ in **four** settings —
> governance package, publication authorisation, detection timing and rollback capability —
> so the difference in outcome follows from the combined organisational and technical
> assumptions the packages assert, and from the definitions of those packages, not from
> legal coverage alone. No enacted statute is represented.
